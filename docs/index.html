1. The Civil Clock vs. Solar Meridian Problem

Classical BaZi partitions each solar day into twelve two-hour Earthly Branch windows (ShiChen), anchored to local solar noon—the exact moment the sun crosses the observer's meridian. Modern wall clocks introduce three compounding errors:

  • Time-Zone Longitude Offset: Every 1° difference between birth longitude and the political zone's standard meridian shifts true solar noon by 4 minutes.
  • Daylight Saving Time (DST): Artificial +60 minute summer shifts have zero astronomical basis and push ~50% of summer births into the wrong Hour Pillar if unstripped.
  • Equation of Time (EoT): Earth's orbital eccentricity and axial tilt shift solar noon between -14.2 minutes (February) and +16.4 minutes (November).
True Solar Time = Civil Clock Time - DST_Offset + 4 min × (Birth_Longitude - Zone_Meridian) + Equation_of_Time(Day)
